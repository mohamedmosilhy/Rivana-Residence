# Review, m9-payments-and-cancellations (M9-T8), 2026-11-25

**Scope**: 41 files (27 modified, 14 untracked), uncommitted
**Verdict**: Changes requested

## Summary
M9-T8 adds staff decisions on cancellation requests (S17 to S19), the paid S9 path, `CompleteRefund`, a post-commit `RefundDispatcher`, refund retry (S45), the payments list (S44), refund callbacks and the 10-minute refund lookup in reconciliation, and the double-charge alert. The core money logic is sound. Every refund result is checked against `refundAttemptKey(payment.id, refundAttempt)` under the reservation lock, writes are conditional (`status: REFUND_PENDING`, expected attempt), event records are idempotent, and all callers share one refund path. Three issues should be fixed first. A booking can be checked in (or marked no-show) while its cancellation refund is pending, and the refund then completes with no alert to anyone. The S18 route rejects the empty body that its own contract documents as optional. The non-obvious `CompleteRefund` branches have no tests.

## Major

### 🟠 A refund pending for a cancellation can complete for a checked-in guest with no alert, `src/application/reservations/complete-refund.ts:207`
**Problem**: Once a paid cancellation is approved (S18 or paid S9), the booking stays `CONFIRMED` with `paymentStatus REFUND_PENDING` and an active request in `REFUND_PENDING`. Nothing stops the front desk from checking the guest in or marking a no-show in the meantime. `reservation-front-desk.ts:154` runs the state machine, and `CHECK_IN` / `MARK_NO_SHOW` (`state-machine.ts:142,151`) do not look at `paymentStatus`. `closeCancellationRequest` closes only `OPEN` requests, so the `REFUND_PENDING` request survives. When the gateway later confirms, `CompleteRefund` marks the payment `REFUNDED`, sets the reservation `paymentStatus REFUNDED`, completes the request, writes `cancelled: false`, and returns. No staff alert and no guest message go out. `RetryRefund` (`retry-refund.ts:89`) also starts a new refund whatever the reservation status is.
**Why it matters**: A guest who is in the hotel, or has already stayed, gets a full refund, and the only trace is one audit row. The task card names this "booking moved on" outcome as a deliberate choice, but the silent part is what turns it into lost money.
**Suggested fix**: Choose one of two fixes, as an owner decision. (a) Refuse `CHECK_IN` (and possibly `MARK_NO_SHOW`) while an active cancellation request is `APPROVED`, `REFUND_PENDING` or `REFUND_FAILED`, or while `paymentStatus` is `REFUND_PENDING`. Check this under the reservation lock in the front-desk use case. (b) At minimum, have `CompleteRefund` queue a branch staff alert in the `cancelled === false` branch. In both cases, have `RetryRefund` refuse, or at least warn, when the booking is no longer `CONFIRMED`, `CANCELLED` or `PAYMENT_FAILED`. Add a test for whichever option is chosen.

### 🟠 S18 documents an optional body but the route rejects an empty one, `src/app/api/v1/staff/cancellation-requests/[id]/approval/route.ts:8`
**Problem**: The contract marks the approval body optional (`requestOptional: true`, `src/contracts/api/v1/routes.ts:518`), so OpenAPI publishes `required: false`. The route does not pass `bodyOptional: true` to `staffRoute`, unlike `no-show` and `pms-entry`. A POST with no body therefore fails JSON parsing (`400 MALFORMED_JSON`) before it reaches the use case. The route test always sends `{}`, so this was never exercised.
**Why it matters**: The published contract is broken for the most common approval call, an approval without a note. The M10 screens will be built against the OpenAPI file.
**Suggested fix**: Add `bodyOptional: true` to the route options, and add a route test that posts with no body and expects 200 or 202.

### 🟠 The non-obvious `CompleteRefund` and dispatcher branches are untested, `tests/integration/reservations/refunds.test.ts`
**Problem**: Tests are configured and this is a Critical task. These branches have no test:
- the "booking moved on" branch (`complete-refund.ts:207-245`, `cancelled: false`, no cancellation, request `COMPLETED`);
- `CompleteRefund` with no waiting request, which is the automatic late-success refund now finished by the dispatcher's immediate answer, by callbacks and by lookups. Success sets `paymentStatus REFUNDED` on a `CANCELLED` or `PAYMENT_FAILED` booking. Failure sets `PAID` and queues `refund.staff-alert`. `payment-callback.test.ts:321,606` still only check the pending stamp;
- a failed refund after a paid staff cancellation with `notifyGuest: true`. Spec 0006 Decision 7 says a refund failure sends nothing to the guest. Only the success path is checked (`refunds.test.ts:487`);
- the `RefundDispatcher` timeout (`send-refund.ts:57-65`), and the S45 route answering `202` (only the `409` is tested);
- an S17 query with an explicit `branchId` (foreign branch → 403 for a manager).
**Why it matters**: These are the state transitions on the money path that a later change is most likely to break without anyone noticing, and the first two decide what the guest and the hotel are told.
**Suggested fix**: Add integration cases for each item. The fixtures (`confirmed()`, `refundCommands`, `paymentJobs`) already make them cheap.

## Minor

### 🟡 S44 returns a `provider` field that the strict contract does not declare, `src/app/api/v1/staff/reservations/[id]/payments/route.ts:16`
**Problem**: `...row` spreads `PaymentView`, which includes `provider` (`booking-payment-store.ts:220`). The `Payment` schema in `src/contracts/api/v1/cancellation-requests.ts:102` is a `strictObject` without `provider`.
**Why it matters**: The response and the OpenAPI document disagree, and any response validation or generated client will reject or drop the field.
**Suggested fix**: Either add `provider` to the schema (it is the stored adapter name, which is acceptable for staff) or map the fields explicitly in the route.

### 🟡 Errors after commit turn a committed decision into a 500, `src/application/reservations/send-refund.ts:71`
**Problem**: `RefundDispatcher.send` catches gateway errors but not a failure of its own stamping transaction (`unitOfWork.run` → `markRefundRequested`), for example a lock timeout or a dropped connection. The error propagates through `DecideCancellationRequest` (`decide-cancellation-request.ts:352`), `CancelPaidReservation` (`cancel-paid-reservation.ts:195`) and `RetryRefund` (`retry-refund.ts:146`). The decision has already committed, so the client sees a 500, and a retry then gets `409 INVALID_TRANSITION`. The sweep resends the same key later, so no money is wrong.
**Suggested fix**: Catch errors around the stamp, the same way the `complete.execute` call is already wrapped, and still return the committed result. The unstamped attempt is then left to the sweep, as the docstring already promises.

### 🟡 The `refund.staff-alert` wording is wrong for automatic refunds and bookings that moved on, `src/infrastructure/email/reservation-email.ts:62`
**Problem**: The alert always says "The booking is still confirmed." A failed automatic refund (late success, Q-63) belongs to a `CANCELLED` or `PAYMENT_FAILED` booking, and after a check-in the booking is `CHECKED_IN`.
**Suggested fix**: Drop the sentence, or pass the reservation status, or a flag, in the payload and word the alert to match.

### 🟡 The guest detail shows staff-created requests, even with `notifyGuest: false`, `src/infrastructure/db/prisma/repositories/reservations/cancellation-request-store.ts:54`
**Problem**: `findCurrent` (used by G1 and G4) returns any active or latest request, including `requestedBy STAFF`. A paid staff cancellation therefore appears to the guest as "their" cancellation request in `REFUND_PENDING` or `REFUND_FAILED`, with `decisionReason` (the `REASON_TEXT`) when `notifyGuest` was true. This sits uneasily with "a refund failure sends nothing to the guest". The task card also accepts storing `notifyGuest` as "decisionReason is not null", which ties the guest-facing text to a flag.
**Suggested fix**: Filter `findCurrent` to `requestedBy: "GUEST"`, or map staff requests to a neutral guest view. Record the `decisionReason` encoding in the spec notes as a known shortcut to replace when a migration next touches the table.

### 🟡 One WhatsApp purpose for two payload shapes, `src/domain/notifications/whatsapp-template.ts:91`
**Problem**: `CANCELLATION_APPROVED` allows `refundAmount`, but unpaid approvals queue no `refundAmount` (`reservation-messages.ts:100-102`). If staff set up the template with `{{refundAmount}}`, every unpaid approval WhatsApp fails to render. If they set it up without the variable, paid approvals lose the amount.
**Suggested fix**: Document that the template must not require `refundAmount`, or send a placeholder value, or split the purpose (approved-cancelled versus approved-refund-pending) in a later task.

### 🟡 The concurrency cases do not observe lock waits, `tests/integration/reservations/refunds.test.ts:681`
**Problem**: Both 20-round races (`:682`, `:710`) run `Promise.all` on independent clients but never prove that the transactions overlapped. `tests/integration/reservations/AGENTS.md` requires lock waits observed through `pg_stat_activity` and states that sleeps or plain parallelism are not evidence.
**Suggested fix**: Use the barrier and lock-wait helpers in `support/concurrency.ts`, as `payment-jobs.test.ts` does.

### 🟡 A refund lookup that returns an older attempt's answer counts as "answered" on every run, `src/application/reservations/reconcile-payments.ts:199`
**Problem**: When `getTransaction` returns the refund event for an earlier attempt, `CompleteRefund` ignores it, but the item is counted as `answered`. The payment then stays `REFUND_PENDING` and is looked up on every run, and staff get no signal that the refund is stuck.
**Suggested fix**: Count it as `pending` when `applied` is false, which needs a result from `applyVerifiedEvent`. Consider a staff alert for a refund pending longer than N hours (follow-up).

### 🟡 Paid S9 on a `CANCELLED` booking whose automatic refund failed returns 409 instead of the replay 200, `src/application/reservations/cancel-reservation.ts:107`
**Problem**: After a failed automatic refund, the booking is `CANCELLED` with `paymentStatus PAID`. The paid branch runs before the `CANCELLED` replay check and answers `409 INVALID_TRANSITION`, while every other cancelled booking replays 200.
**Suggested fix**: Return the replay for `CANCELLED` before choosing the paid path, or confirm the 409 is intended.

## Strengths
- The attempt-key rule is applied exactly as spec 0006 Decision 7 says. `CompleteRefund.applyLocked` re-reads the payment under the reservation lock and acts only when it is `REFUND_PENDING` and the key matches the current `refundAttempt`. The callback, the lookup and the immediate answer all go through it, and the stale-attempt and double-delivery tests prove it.
- One refund path. Decisions, paid staff cancellation, retry, the late-success automatic refund and the sweep all use `RefundDispatcher` (network call after commit, 15 s timeout, stamp only for the sent attempt). `startRefund` resets `refundRequestedAt`, so a late stamp for an old attempt is a no-op.
- The lock rule holds. Every new path locks only the reservation row (releases are allowed), with no listing lock after it. Audit rows commit in the same transaction as every money and request change, and nothing new is logged.
- The double-charge alert fires only on first sight of the event (`earlier === null`), so gateway retries do not repeat it. It is audited and tested.
- Authorization stays in the use cases (`authorize` plus `authorizeBranch`), a foreign branch is hidden as `NOT_FOUND`, and the role sweep covers unauthenticated, receptionist, editor and outsider.

## Test coverage
Coverage is good for the main paths on real PostgreSQL: rejection; unpaid and paid approval; immediate success, pending then callback, failure then retry with a new key; an unsent refund recovered by the sweep with the same key; the lookup after 10 minutes; a mismatched amount; idempotent `CompleteRefund`; paid S9 with and without `notifyGuest`; superseding an open guest request; the AC-8 role sweep; S44 branch scoping; the double-charge alert; two 20-round races; and route status codes (200, 202, 403, 401, 409, 422).
