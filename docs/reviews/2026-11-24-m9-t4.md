## Summary

An online booking commits its room hold, payment, coupon and held confirmations before asking the gateway for a checkout. Both website and staff bookings can expire an overdue hold under the listing locks. The initial review found one race involving a definite gateway refusal and a gap in the notification tests.

## Original blocker

### 🔴 A concurrent retry could create a checkout after a definite refusal, `src/application/reservations/website-checkout.ts:104`

**Problem.** Two matching requests could reach the gateway while the payment had no stored checkout URL. The fake consumed a scripted refusal once. The second call could open a session before the first call acquired the reservation lock and committed the failure.

**Impact.** The booking could release its room and coupon and queue a failure notice while the gateway still had a live checkout for that payment.

**Resolution.** The gateway contract now reserves `PROVIDER_REJECTED` for a final refusal for that payment ID. An adapter that cannot establish that guarantee returns `UNAVAILABLE`, which preserves the hold. The fake keeps a scripted final refusal for that ID. The shared contract checks repeated and concurrent calls. The PostgreSQL regression overlaps a fresh request and a matching replay and checks compensation under the reservation lock.

## Original minor finding

### 🟡 The failure notice lacked dispatcher coverage, `src/application/notifications/dispatch-outbox.ts:22`

**Problem.** The tests checked queued rows and email wording separately. An incorrect worker mapping for the WhatsApp purpose or a rejected deadline payload could pass both tests.

**Resolution.** The expiry test now runs the worker against the committed outbox. It verifies the email event, WhatsApp purpose and deadline, both scrubbed terminal payloads, and zero dispatch attempts on all three held confirmations.
